The store's app: what it does, and what it runs on

Everything the app covers, the services behind it, where the store's data physically sits, what it costs a month, and the short list of things only you can do.

Robinson's General Store · for Ravi Kiran · 11 August 2026

What the app covers

Seven areas. Everything starts from a photograph of a piece of paper, which is the part that used to get lost.

Every area listed here is built and in use. The staff see only what their role allows.
Area What it does
Capture Photograph or drop an invoice and the app reads the vendor, date, and each line off it. Staff confirm one screen. Anything the app was unsure about is flagged in amber and cannot be saved until a person looks at it. There is a typing path too, for phone orders with no paperwork.
Vendors and orders The vendor directory by department, each vendor's orders, invoices, credits, contact and rep, payment terms, and the ordering profile: minimum order, when the summer order goes in, where it gets placed, and whether it needed reordering.
Money Invoices with HST handled four ways, freight, part deliveries, payments split across several invoices, deposits, post-dated cheques that settle themselves on the day, credit notes, and what is due or overdue with a quick way to pay it.
Stock and pricing Inventory counts, reorder suggestions worked out from the order history and the counts, and printable Garden Centre price signs with a margin calculator.
Knowing and asking The knowledge notes, where what used to live in somebody's head gets written down and attributed, and Ask the store, which answers questions from the store's own records and names where the answer came from.
Property and people Building assets and recurring maintenance tasks with due dates and a daily checklist, the Ontario pesticide licence and insurance with their expiry dates, employees, pay rates, and the weekly schedule.
Getting data in and out Import the bookings workbook, the weekly schedule or a stock sheet; export everything as one Excel file with a tab per table. Plus the Suggestions space where your notes, screenshots and voice recordings become work.

The three places the app reads and writes for you

  • Reading invoices. Turns a photograph into lines with amounts, flagging anything it is unsure of.
  • Answering questions. Ask the store anything about your vendors, invoices and notes, in plain words.
  • Drafting the reorder list. Suggests what to reorder, with the reasoning shown, for you to accept or ignore.

None of the three decides anything on its own. Every dollar figure is confirmed by a person before it is saved.

Web addresses

  • The store's own address: app.robinsonsgeneralstore.ca. Live, secure, and the one to use and share. Checked 11 August 2026.
  • The backup address: robinsons-store.vercel.app. Kept on purpose, and it cannot be switched off in any case. It does not depend on the store's domain or on Vianet, so if the domain ever lapses or a setting is changed by mistake, the app stays reachable here and staff can carry on receiving stock.

Both reach the same app and the same data. There is no second copy and nothing to keep in step. Use the store's own address on anything printed or shared, and make sure the manager knows the backup one exists.

Where the store's data actually sits

This matters for a Canadian business, so it was chosen deliberately rather than by default.

Corrected 24 October 2026. An earlier version of this section said only invoice photos go to the AI and that staff details never do. Both were wrong. This is the full list.

  • The store's records are kept in Canada. Vendors, orders, invoices, payments, staff records and every uploaded photo are stored in the Toronto data centre.
  • Each AI feature sends some of it to the United States. The AI is run by Anthropic, a US company, and it only sees what a feature sends when someone uses it. Reading an invoice sends the photo. Asking the store sends the question, the knowledge notes staff have typed, vendor names, phone numbers and notes, and, for anyone allowed to see money, invoice amounts and due dates. The reorder summary sends vendors, orders with their amounts, and reorder notes. A suggestion with a screenshot sends the screenshot and the note, so the AI can describe it. A change to the app requested in the store's Slack channel sends the request text along with the app's code.
  • Anthropic deletes what it receives within 30 days. It keeps it longer only if something is flagged as misuse or the law requires it. Its business terms do not allow it to train the AI on the store's data.
  • Staff details can reach the AI through a screenshot. The AI never reads the staff, pay or schedule records on its own. A screenshot of those screens is a different matter: it goes as a picture, so leave pay, schedules and staff details out of any screenshot you attach. The Suggestions page now says this under the screenshot box. Personal details typed into a knowledge note also go to the AI whenever someone asks a question.
  • The daily email goes through the United States too. When it is switched on, Resend sends vendor names, invoice numbers and amounts owed. Resend has no Canadian location and keeps its records for 30 days.
  • Records are kept for six years, which is the Canadian requirement for business records.

What protects it

  • The database enforces the rules, not just the screens. Staff cannot reach cost, order amounts, payments or pay even by going around the app. The rule sits in the database itself.
  • Every login belongs to one store and one role. Owner, manager, lead, staff.
  • Nothing is ever truly deleted. Deleting marks a record as void and hides it. It stays recoverable, and the export includes it.
  • Every change records who and when. Every save writes an entry naming the person.
  • Invoice photos are private. Links to them expire. There is no permanent public address for an invoice image.
  • Money cannot be edited by hand. Payments go through one controlled path, so an invoice can never show "paid" with no payment behind it.
  • The app checks itself after every update. It confirms the app and the database still agree on what each invoice owes, and raises a ticket automatically if they do not.
  • You can take everything out at any time. The Import and Export screen produces one Excel workbook with a tab per table.

Two copies: a practice one and the real one

You asked on 31 July to try changes before they reach the store. That is how it now works.

  • The practice site has its own separate database filled with invented data. Anything can be tried there and nothing real is at risk.
  • The live site is what the store uses.
  • A change reaches the store only when you say so. It is built, it appears on the practice site, you try it, and moving the ticket to Approved is what sends it live.
  • Real data never copies into the practice site, and practice data never reaches the live one.
